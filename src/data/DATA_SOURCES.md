# FITX data sources

## Exercise library

Exercise names, instructions, muscle groups, equipment, and related text are adapted from [Free Exercise DB](https://github.com/yuhonas/free-exercise-db), revision `f00c92c7dcf1216a928a52c3706c7ce8e2f71ed5`. Its repository publishes the dataset under the [Unlicense](https://github.com/yuhonas/free-exercise-db/blob/f00c92c7dcf1216a928a52c3706c7ce8e2f71ed5/UNLICENSE). FITX imports the text fields into `exercise_library`. The Phase 2 library resolves reference stills from the same pinned revision, on demand; it does not mirror them. These are sequential still images, not animated demonstrations, and unavailable frames fall back to a labeled placeholder.

## Food nutrition

These records are informational. Food entries can be incomplete; check package labels for allergy and dietary decisions.

Food search looks in FITX's own food catalogue (the `food_catalog` and `food_portions` tables, filled from USDA FoodData Central) and the user's own foods. The built-in list (the FITX staples and the USDA Foundation Foods snapshot below) answers for any food the catalogue did not return: before the catalogue migrations are applied, while its seed parts are being applied, and for foods the seed does not hold. A food in both is listed once, with the catalogue's values. Live USDA search adds results only when the server has `USDA_API_KEY` and FITX found little. Open Food Facts is used only for barcodes and when the user asks to search packaged products. Each search reports which sources answered ("FITX food catalogue", "FITX built-in foods", "Your foods" and so on), and no answer is cached by the browser, because answers include the user's own foods.

### FITX food catalogue (`supabase/migrations/20261118140010_*` onwards)

The catalogue seed migrations are generated by `scripts/import-fdc.mjs` from USDA FoodData Central (public domain, CC0 1.0), read either from FDC's bulk JSON downloads or from saved API search pages; the header of each part says which:

- **Survey (FNDDS)**, published 31 October 2024 (bulk file `FoodData_Central_survey_food_json_2024-10-31.zip`, or the API's `{"query":"*","dataType":["Survey (FNDDS)"]}` pages), with FDC's household measures as portions. FNDDS values are typical recipes (value basis `recipe_calculated`, shown as "Typical recipe · estimate").
- **Foundation**: Foundation foods, laboratory-analysed (`lab_analysed`). Foundation foods that give no kcal value use FDC's Atwater energy (specific factors, else general). The bulk file also gives their measured portions; the labelling reference amount (RACC) is left out.
- **SR Legacy**: only the curated staples below (`compiled`), whose portions were read from full FDC records.
- **Left out**: infant formula, baby food and human milk; records without energy, protein, carbohydrate and fat; values outside 0–950 kcal or more than 101 g of macros per 100 g; and the two FNDDS records excluded on review (paneer and pakora, below).
- **Portions**: FDC's own measures that start with "1" ("1 cup, cooked", "1 item", "1 medium"). Mass measures ("1 oz, cooked"), "Quantity not specified", "1 surface inch" and survey wording such as "Guideline amount per fl oz" are left out. Plain measures ("1 cup", "1 cup, cooked") come before variants ("1 cup, melted"). A bare count ("2 rotis") uses the only piece measure or the medium one, never a container, can, carton, packet, pack or cubic inch (nor an Open Food Facts product's whole pack): then FITX asks for grams.
- **Search words**: FITX aliases (exact names that can select a food, from the staples) and FNDDS's "additional descriptions" as keywords, which help find a food but never select it (FNDDS lists "lobster" under Seafood sauce).
- **Diet labels** (`src/lib/food/diet.ts`): "Non-veg" and "Contains egg" come from the food's name, category and FNDDS ingredient list. Dishes that are meat without naming it (hamburgers, cheeseburgers, Whoppers, Big Macs, hot dogs, corn dogs, pigs in a blanket) count when they are the food or one of its ingredients ("Hot dog, NFS"), not when they only describe a bun ("Rolls, hamburger or hotdog"). A food that says it is vegetarian or meatless is never marked non-veg. FITX never claims "vegetarian" from those lists, because an ingredient can be a product whose contents its name does not show (marshmallows contain gelatin). Only the hand-checked staples carry "Vegetarian" or "Vegan". The built-in list uses the same rules, so a food carries the same label in both, and catalogue rows saved under older rules never show a weaker label than their name and category give now. The search page's diet filter ("Hide foods marked non-veg", "Hide foods marked egg", ticked from the user's diet) hides only labelled foods and shows the rest as "Diet not checked".
- **Indian dishes**: FNDDS dishes such as idli, dosa, sambar, dal, roti, paratha, puri, naan, upma, vada, samosa, biryani, palak paneer and channa saag are tagged `cuisine = indian`. They are USDA's typical recipes and are labelled as estimates. The Indian Food Composition Tables (IFCT 2017) and the Indian Nutrient Databank are not used: their licences need written permission.

Current seed: built from the API pages fetched on 16 November 2026 before the USDA demo key's daily limit ran out. It has 2,124 foods: 1,941 FNDDS foods from records 1–2,000 of 5,428 by FDC id (dairy, meat, poultry, fish, eggs and most pulses), 172 Foundation foods from the first 200 of 424, and 11 SR Legacy staples. Fruit, vegetables, grains, drinks and most Indian dishes are not in it yet beyond the 33 staples. To complete it, download `FoodData_Central_survey_food_json_2024-10-31.zip` (3.8 MB) and the newest `FoodData_Central_foundation_food_json_*.zip` (0.5 MB) from <https://fdc.nal.usda.gov/download-datasets> into one folder, then run `node --import ./tests/setup/hooks.mjs scripts/import-fdc.mjs <that folder> --append`. `--append` keeps parts 01–05 byte for byte and adds only the missing foods as new parts after them, so a database that already applied 01–05 needs only the new parts. Without downloads, `USDA_API_KEY=<api.data.gov key> node scripts/import-fdc-fetch.mjs <folder>` saves the API pages instead (resuming where it stopped; about a minute with a free key, a day or more on `DEMO_KEY`), followed by the same importer command. (Without `--append` the importer rewrites every part, which is right only while no part has been applied anywhere.)

Applying it: each part (about 150 KB) is a plain SQL file that adds only the foods the catalogue does not have yet, with their portions, so running it again changes nothing and never undoes a later change (a food hidden or corrected since stays that way). Corrections to foods already applied ship as their own migrations. Apply `20261118140000`, `20261118140001` and `20261118140002` first, then the parts in order, ideally in one session. Search keeps answering from the built-in list for anything the catalogue does not hold yet, so no food is lost while parts are missing. The parts are ordered so the first ones hold the curated staples, Indian dishes and Foundation foods. Large parts are easiest to apply with the Supabase CLI (`supabase db push`) or the SQL editor; see the Phase 5 hand-over for details.

### FITX staples (`src/data/fitx-staples.json`)

A small, curated list of cooked staples, common Indian foods and everyday basics (eggs, milk, oats, onion, tomato, olive oil, honey, butter, chickpeas, pasta), taken only from [USDA FoodData Central](https://fdc.nal.usda.gov/) (FDC). FDC data is public domain (CC0 1.0); the suggested citation is: U.S. Department of Agriculture, Agricultural Research Service. FoodData Central. fdc.nal.usda.gov.

- **Retrieved** on 16 November 2026 from the FDC API: the search endpoint (`POST https://api.nal.usda.gov/fdc/v1/foods/search`) for Survey (FNDDS) foods and their household measures, and the foods endpoint (`POST https://api.nal.usda.gov/fdc/v1/foods`, full records) for SR Legacy and Foundation foods and their portions. A script copied the values out of the API responses; nothing was typed by hand. Each entry keeps its `fdcId`, data type, food code (NDB number for SR Legacy and Foundation) and FDC publication date, so any value can be rechecked at `https://fdc.nal.usda.gov/food-details/<fdcId>/nutrients` or with `GET /v1/food/<fdcId>`.
- **Data types:** Survey (FNDDS) foods, published in FDC on 31 October 2024; SR Legacy foods (dated 1 April 2019 in FDC) for parboiled rice, raw and hard-boiled egg, dry oats, raw onion, raw tomato, olive oil, honey, salted butter, cooked chickpeas and dry pasta; and one Foundation food, whole milk (dated 16 December 2019), which replaces the same food in the Foundation snapshot so it is listed once.
- **Values** are per 100 g: energy is FDC nutrient 208 (kcal), protein 203, carbohydrate by difference 205, total fat 204.
- **Portions** are only the weights FDC publishes for that food, such as "1 item" = 38 g for idli and "1 cup, cooked" = 158 g for white rice. FNDDS "Quantity not specified" and "1 surface inch" measures are left out. FDC cups and tablespoons are US measures (236.6 ml and 14.8 ml).
- **Bare counts** ("2 rotis") use the portion marked `countDefault`: "1 medium" for roti, dosa, onion and tomato, "1 large" for eggs (USDA's standard egg), "1 regular/large" for samosa, the whole 10-inch naan, and the only piece weight for the rest. A typed size ("2 large rotis", "2 medium eggs") picks FDC's matching size; when FDC weighs other sizes but not that one ("2 small boiled eggs", where FDC only gives a large one), the app asks for grams.
- **State** comes from the FDC description: cooked staples, raw egg, onion, tomato, dry oats and dry pasta, and none for ghee, milk, oil, honey and butter. For grains and pulses, search ranks cooked forms before raw ones.
- **Aliases** are FITX's own search spellings (idly, chapathi, sambhar, daal, anda, pyaz) plus FDC's additional descriptions (FNDDS lists "macaroni, spaghetti, any shape of pasta" for Pasta, cooked). They are not nutrition data. No two staples share an alias, so a typed name points to one food; plain "biryani" is deliberately no staple's alias, because vegetable, chicken and meat biryani all answer to it.

Searches used (all Survey (FNDDS) unless noted):
1. `idli dosa sambar upma poha paneer chapati roti dal`
2. `rice cooked`, Survey (FNDDS) and SR Legacy, all words required
3. `paratha naan puri biryani khichdi chana rajma samosa pakora kheer raita lassi korma tikka tandoori vada pongal pulao poha chole aloo ghee curd`
4. `pasta cooked`, Survey (FNDDS) and SR Legacy, all words required

Full records fetched by `fdcId` (SR Legacy unless noted): 171287 egg, whole, raw, fresh; 173424 egg, whole, cooked, hard-boiled; 173904 oats, regular and quick, dry; 170000 onions, raw; 170457 tomatoes, red, ripe, raw; 171413 olive oil; 169640 honey; 173410 butter, salted; 173757 chickpeas, cooked, boiled; 169736 pasta, dry, enriched; and Foundation 746782 whole milk. Foundation records for egg, rolled oats, red onion and roma tomato were compared too; they are already in the Foundation snapshot and only the milk record carries a household measure, so the SR Legacy versions with FDC's piece, cup and spoon weights were used for the rest.

Searched for but not found among FNDDS foods: poha, khichdi, rajma, pongal, kheer, raita, lassi, korma, tikka, tandoori, pulao and chole.

Left out on purpose:
- **FNDDS "Cheese, paneer"** (fdcId 2705740, food code 14110050). Its recipe is 97 g whole milk plus 3 g vinegar, concentrated with all the milk sugar kept, which gives 22.5 g carbohydrate and 15.9 g protein per 100 g. Real paneer loses that sugar with the whey, so the entry misstates both. Until a better source is added (Phase 5), paneer comes from package labels. "Palak Paneer" (2709631) is kept: its recipe uses cottage cheese, not that entry.
- **FNDDS "Pakora"** (fdcId 2710066): not a staple, and 125 kcal per 100 g is low for a fried snack. Left for the Phase 5 review.

Worth knowing about values that are kept:
- "Upma" (87 kcal per 100 g) is a moist recipe of cream of wheat, onion, tomato, oil and water; drier home recipes are higher.
- "Biryani with chicken" (104 kcal per 100 g) is a home recipe with potato and yogurt; restaurant biryani is usually richer.
- "Dal" (145 kcal per 100 g) is boiled lentils with a little ghee.

### How a search picks a food

`src/lib/domain/food-query.ts` ranks the foods that match every typed word. Forms the search did not name rank below the plain food: dried, powder, flour, concentrate, breaded, fried, paste, puree, sauce, milk, butter, juice and oil, when the word leads the description ("Oat milk", "Tomato, paste", "Onion rings, breaded"), but not as a later detail ("Yogurt, plain, whole milk"). So "oats" lists oats before oat milk, and "almonds" lists almonds before almond butter.

When the search includes an amount, the app selects a food and fills in the amount only when exactly one food's whole name or alias is what was typed ("2 eggs", "100 g oats", "1 tbsp honey"); one of the user's own custom foods named exactly that wins. Otherwise ("200 g paneer", "100 g almonds", "100 g biryani") it keeps the amount and asks the user to tap the food they mean.

### How amounts are worked out

`src/lib/domain/food-query.ts` reads the amount out of what the user types ("3 idlis", "150 g rice", "1.5 cups dal").
- Grams, kilograms, ounces and pounds are exact conversions.
- Pieces and cups use the food's FDC portion weight, and the app labels the result a **portion estimate** with the grams it used.
- Teaspoons, tablespoons, ml and litres are converted from the food's nearest FDC volume measure in US units (for example, 2 tsp of ghee from FDC's 1 tablespoon = 14 g). These are also portion estimates.
- When a unit has no known weight for that food, such as a katori, bowl, plate or glass, or a count for a food without a piece weight, the app asks for grams instead of guessing.

### USDA Foundation Foods snapshot (`src/data/usda-foundation-foods.json`)

A compact selection from USDA FoodData Central Foundation Foods, April 2026, with values per 100 g, used by the built-in list until the catalogue is applied. FITX reads "raw" or "cooked" from each description and shows it as a label. "Dry" counts as raw only for grains, pulses and pasta, so "Lentils, dry" is labelled raw while "Cheese, dry white" is not; "baked, from raw" counts as cooked.

### Live USDA search (optional)

When the server has `USDA_API_KEY`, search also asks the FDC API for Survey (FNDDS), Foundation, SR Legacy and Branded foods. The key is sent in the `X-Api-Key` header, never in the URL. FNDDS household measures and Branded serving sizes in grams become portions.

### Open Food Facts

Barcode results and the packaged-products search come from [Open Food Facts](https://world.openfoodfacts.org/), whose database is under the ODbL and whose images are under CC BY-SA ([terms](https://world.openfoodfacts.org/terms-of-use)). Its values are entered by the community from package labels; FITX labels them "Package label (Open Food Facts)" and shows product photos only from Open Food Facts' image hosts, each with the credit "Photo: Open Food Facts contributors, CC BY-SA" and a link to the product page. FITX calls it only for a barcode or when the user asks to search packaged products, because all FITX users share the server's per-IP limit (about 15 product reads and 10 searches a minute). Packaged searches look at products sold in India first, and worldwide only when there are none. Product reads are cached for a day and searches for an hour.
- A barcode is checked before any lookup: EAN-13, EAN-8, UPC-A and UPC-E (expanded to UPC-A) with a valid check digit. The user's own food with that barcode comes before Open Food Facts.
- Not found, no usable label, busy (HTTP 429 or 503), no answer and timed out each get their own message. When a product is missing or has no usable label, the user can save it as their own food with the barcode filled in.
- Products offer "1 serving" and "1 pack" portions from their label when those are given in grams.
- Only per-100 g fields are used, because the plain energy field can be per serving.
- Labels that give only kJ are converted at 4.184 kJ per kcal. A result above 900 kcal per 100 g is kept only when the label's own nutrients account for it.
- A declared 0 kcal (water, diet drinks) is kept, unless the same label lists more than 1 g of protein, carbohydrate and fat per 100 g, which marks an entry error.
- Contributors sometimes type the kJ figure into the kcal field, which would log 4.2 times too much. A kcal value that the label's own protein, carbohydrate, fat, fibre and alcohol account for (4, 4, 9, 2 and 7 kcal per gram, within about 15%) is kept, even when the kJ field holds the same number, and even above 900 per 100 g, since US labels round per serving (110 kcal in a 12 g tablespoon of shortening is 917 per 100 g). Otherwise a kcal value that equals the kJ value, is above 900 per 100 g (pure fat is about 900), or is far above what the label's nutrients allow is read as kJ when that fits those nutrients, and the product is left out of the results when it doesn't. When a label lists too few nutrients to check against, a kcal value equal to its kJ value is read as kJ, and one above 900 is left out.
- A protein, carbohydrate or fat value the label leaves blank counts as unknown, not 0 g, so "far above the nutrients" applies only to labels that list all three: paneer with 288 kcal, 18.3 g protein, 1.2 g carbohydrate and no fat value keeps its 288 kcal.
- A label whose protein, carbohydrate and fat add up to more than 101 g per 100 g (1 g allowed for label rounding) is left out.
- The User-Agent comes from `OPEN_FOOD_FACTS_USER_AGENT`. Open Food Facts asks for "AppName/Version (contact email)"; a value without a contact gets `(https://fitnessx.vercel.app)` added, and the default is `FITX/1.0 (https://fitnessx.vercel.app)`.
